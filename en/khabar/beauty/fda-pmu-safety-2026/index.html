Permanent Makeup Pigments: FDA Safety Points for Consumers

FDA guidance on permanent makeup highlights key safety considerations, from skin reactions and difficult removal to single-use tools and contaminated ink.

Written by: Lena Bahri Beauty Journal Editorial TeamReviewed by: Lena BahriOctober 10, 20264 min read

FDA guidance on tattoos and permanent makeup highlights pigment reactions, difficult removal, infection transmission from non-sterile equipment and contaminated ink as issues consumers should understand before deciding.

What does the FDA tell consumers?

The FDA Q&A page mentions adverse reactions to ink, lasting color changes after removal, disease transmission through non-sterile needles and infections from contaminated ink. It also notes that ink contamination has been reported in some cases despite hygienic practices by tattoo artists.

This guidance does not guarantee the safety of any specific method or brand; it outlines precautions to support informed decisions.

What does this U.S. guidance say about Iran?

The FDA is a U.S. regulatory agency; its information should not be presented as Iranian law or official Iranian licensing. This article does not interpret local Iranian regulations; it summarizes general safety points from an official source.

Before permanent makeup, ask about single-use needles and opening sealed packs in front of you, disinfection, allergy history, pigment ingredients and aftercare. If you have a skin condition or medical concern, consult the appropriate doctor.

Source and Date

The primary source is the FDA consumer page “Cosmetics Safety Q&A: Tattoos and Permanent Makeup.” The page does not state a publication date for the current text; this article’s date does not indicate that FDA guidance is new.
